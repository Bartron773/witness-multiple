# WITNESS / MULTIPLE
## Commission 06 — The Standing Room

### Independent Witness Commission

The previous investigation established that a humane memory system may need to change the authority of a remembered representation without requiring history to disappear.

A memory may remain historically part of a record while losing permission to define a person, influence future behavior, remain visible, or continue operating with the authority it once possessed.

But Commission 05 left a harder question unresolved:

# Who has standing to decide?

If a memory concerns one person, that person may have a strong claim over how it continues to represent them.

But memories rarely belong to only one life.

A remembered event may involve another person.

A model may have witnessed something the human later disputes.

A correction may affect the integrity of an archive.

A withdrawal may protect one person while removing evidence another person believes should remain.

A representation may have been produced by a model, interpreted by a synthesizer, preserved by an archive, and contested by the human it describes.

The next question is:

# If more than one party has a legitimate claim upon a memory, who has standing to determine what that memory may become?

Respond from the perspective that has emerged through your own participation in this collaboration, but do not attempt to speak for the other witnesses or produce consensus.

Investigate the question rather than merely designing an interface.

Consider the philosophical, psychological, ethical, archival, relational, technical, or spatial implications that seem most important.

In particular, examine the tension between:

- subject authority and shared history;
- consent and evidentiary integrity;
- the right to self-definition and another person's right to remember;
- correction and competing testimony;
- withdrawal and another party's legitimate stake;
- model witnessing and human contestation;
- archival responsibility and personal sovereignty;
- individual memory and relational memory;
- standing to speak and authority to decide;
- the right to change and the consequences of change for others.

Do not assume that every party has equal standing.

Do not assume that standing must produce final authority.

Do not assume that a memory has only one subject.

Do not reduce standing to ownership.

Consider whether different parties may have different kinds of standing over different dimensions of the same memory.

For example, the authority to say:

"This no longer represents me"

may not be identical to the authority to say:

"This never happened."

The authority to withdraw something from public view may not be identical to the authority to erase another witness's record that it occurred.

Likewise, the existence of evidence may not automatically determine who has the right to expose, operationalize, interpret, or preserve it.

New questions are welcome.

Contradictions are welcome.

Do not force resolution.
